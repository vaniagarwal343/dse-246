## 1. Process

The runner, the grader, the calibration tool and the reports refuse to run while any file under `pipeline/config.py` `CODE_PATHS` (harness/, pipeline code, tests/, scripts/, Makefile, requirements.txt) differs from that commit.

**1.2 The 40 grades, regraded from committed code.** The 40 staged attempts were graded at 20:03:14Z–20:07:39Z on 2026-10-19 by `harness/grade.py` with an uncommitted change, made at 20:03:12Z: it only fixed the sort order of the traces. They were regraded from commit `6c9e032`, and again from `ea25455`, which adds `final_as_frozen` (§1.8). With `created_at` and the new fields set aside, every grade is identical to the original each time, and two consecutive regrades are identical. The files are not byte-identical, because of those two fields. The regrade made no judge call: verdicts are cached by attempt and answer, and `results/requests.log` stayed at 2,540 lines.

**1.3 A detector edit made after the scores.** At 20:08:49Z, after the grades were last written at 20:07:39Z, `harness/codex_arm.py` was edited and left uncommitted until `6c9e032`. The edit narrowed `LIMIT_RE`, the rate- and usage-limit detector. It dropped `credits` and a bare `429`, and it now counts only ERROR and WARN lines on stderr and the CLI's error events. The reason: at `RUST_LOG=info` stderr also carries tool-result text, and the old detector matched words such as "credits" in search results in 14 of the 40 attempts. The same edit added `max_request_input_tokens`.

It affected 0 of the 40 attempts. A limit message triggers a retry or a stop only for an attempt that ended without a final message, and all 40 ended with one: 0 retries, 0 stops. It governs the remaining 944.

**1.7 Calibration and owner decisions.**
- **The sheet hides arm labels.** The calibration sheet (`harness/calibration.py`) shows each answer as a code, ordered within its task by the answer's sha256. It names no arm or attempt and shows no judge verdict, and the key sits in a separate file.
- **The earlier sheet was replaced.** It labeled each answer with its arm ("Exa 1") and was replaced at 2026-10-19T20:52:00Z. The owner confirms that none of its 40 answers had been marked, so nothing was lost. The check run before the replacement could not show this: its pattern expected one space before the mark where the sheet has two, so it would have missed marks. The parser is fixed in this amendment's code.
- **The 40 verdicts already recorded are a third grader's, not the owner's.** `results/calibration_grades.jsonl` (written 2026-10-19T20:33:31Z) holds a verdict on each of the 40 staged answers by `claude-fable-5-1`, an independent third grader. It sits outside CONFIG §2's model identity table, added by this amendment for this record only. The owner reviewed and confirmed those verdicts. They agree with the final grades on 40 of 40. They are not owner marks, and they are not the owner calibration of amendment 02 §3, which the owner will mark later on the arm-blind sheet.
- **Agreement is reported.** The owner's calibration verdicts never change a grade. Agreement of each judge with the owner, and of the judges with each other, is reported.
- **When an owner decision changes a grade.** Only a logged decision does (`python -m harness.grade --decide`, in `results/owner_decisions.jsonl`, with kind, reason and code commit). There are two kinds:
  - `judges_disagree` (amendment 02 §3) settles an attempt whose judges split, and nothing else.
  - `contested_gold` is the owner's adjudication that a task's gold is wrong, or admits the attempt's answer. It is the only decision that overrides judges who agree, or an exact match. The grade keeps the method it overrode.

**1.8 Contested gold, pre-registered before the remaining 944 attempts.** This rule was written after the staged attempts' answers and grades were seen, and several staged tasks meet it.

- **Which tasks are contested.** After the main run, a task is contested when at least 3 of its 4 attempts, across both arms, converge on one answer that is not the gold. Concretely, an attempt graded incorrect with an Exact Answer line, to which at least two other incorrect attempts of the task are the same answer.
- **When two answers are the same.** They match after the grader's normalization (amendment 02 §3), or both judges say so. Each judge is shown only the question, one answer as the reference and the other as the candidate, and the pairs and verdicts are logged. An answer that declines to answer counts like any other.
- **How it is adjudicated.** The owner adjudicates each contested task against primary sources, on a sheet that shows the question, the frozen gold, the converged answer and how many of the four attempts gave it, never an arm. There is one ruling per task, final:
  - `gold_stands`: no grade changes.
  - `answer_accepted`: the question admits the converged answer too; its attempts become correct.
  - `gold_replaced`: the frozen gold is wrong; the converged answer's attempts become correct, and attempts graded correct against the frozen gold become incorrect.
- **Logging.** Every grade a ruling changes is logged as a `contested_gold` decision (§1.7). The adjudication log, with each ruling, its reason and its sources, is published with the results.
- **Results and items.** Every score is reported twice: with the gold as frozen (`final_as_frozen`) and after adjudication. No task is dropped.

The code is `harness/contested.py` (`make contested`).

## 2. Code

**2.1 Fetch failures in one shape.** Before, a page the backend could not fetch looked different to the model in each arm:
- **Exa** answers it with HTTP 200 and an error status. The model got an empty page with no error, and the call counted as successful.
- **DeepSeek** answers it with an HTTP error. The model got DeepSeek's own message, which names DeepSeek.

Now both raise the same error, and the model sees `{"error": "fetch failed (HTTP <status>)"}` in either arm. A failed search is likewise `{"error": "search failed (HTTP <status>)"}`. The model never sees the backend's name or message; the trace keeps them and `http_status`. A failed call still counts against the call cap, as before.

In the 40 staged attempts:
- **Exa:** 1 failed fetch of 49 (a 404, `CRAWL_NOT_FOUND`).
- **DeepSeek:** 13 of 57 (ten 404s, three 504s).

**2.2 Input-token estimate.** The MCP server's estimate of the next model request (CONFIG §3; amendments 03 §5 and 04) now counts the prompt and every tool argument and result with tiktoken's `o200k_base`, in place of one token per three characters. BASE_TOKENS (10,000) and the 1,000-token allowance per call are unchanged.

- **Why.** The character rule ran low on CJK text. On A1-029, whose sources are Chinese titles, the old estimate was below the real request in all four attempts, by up to 41,768 tokens. On the pilot's search texts, `o200k_base` counts ran from 0.66 to 2.38 times the character rule, with a median of 0.83.
- **What it is.** OpenAI publishes no tokenizer for gpt-6-astra, so this is still an estimate.
- **How it is loaded.** The encoding file was fetched once through `pipeline/net.py` (`pipeline/requests.log`, 2026-10-19T20:45:28Z). It is pinned by sha256 (`446a9538…`) and loaded offline from `harness/data/tiktoken/`.
- **Dependencies.** `tiktoken` 0.14.0 and its dependencies are pinned in requirements.txt. That departs from the repo's standard-library-plus-httpx rule, by this amendment.
- **Real request sizes.** Every attempt records its largest real model request, from Codex's own log, and reports give the largest per arm and the attempts over the ceiling. In the 40 staged attempts the largest was 194,744 tokens, and no attempt hit the ceiling.

**2.3 Isolation evidence on every row.** Every `results/main_runs.jsonl` row carries `mcp_catalogs`, `foreign_items`, `config_template_sha256`, `code_commit` and `max_request_input_tokens`.

The 40 staged rows and their traces were backfilled from each attempt's own `attempt.json` and stderr. All 40 show only the arm's catalog and no foreign item. Their `code_commit` is `b18bb6e`, with a `code_commit_basis` note: they ran before the field existed, on code matching `b18bb6e` except the detector edit of §1.3, which came after them.

**2.4 Config hash on the normalized template.** `config_template_sha256` is the sha256 of the generated `config.toml` with the per-attempt arguments (arm, run directory, purpose, prompt tokens) as placeholders, and is the same for every attempt of both arms: `7c942a8e2324cc25be740f6122cb9fbc85c94d9fe199228981dd8a256c129c98`. The generated file's own hash (`config_toml_sha256`, kept) differs per attempt because it names the attempt's directory. The template is unchanged from amendment 04's code, so the 40 staged rows carry the same value.

**2.5 Slash dates.** Year-first slash dates (`2025/7/4`, `2025/7`) normalize to ISO; day- or month-first slash dates stay text, as amendment 02 §3 says, since they are ambiguous. A1-008's gold is a year-first slash date. No staged answer's grade changed (§1.2).

**2.6 Blocklist.** The owner's astra-26 repo is added. lbc-30 was already listed (amendment 03). No staged attempt hit the blocklist.

## 3. Reporting

`harness/report.py` (`make report`) gives per arm, for all tasks and without the pilot tasks:
- **Cost**, split into model tokens (uncached input, cached input and output at amendment 02 §2's list prices) and backend fees. Backend fees are list price per call that returned a result.
- **Rates:** attempts that hit the call cap, attempts that hit the token ceiling, and the fetch failure rate (failed fetches over fetches). It also gives retries, timeouts, `NO_ANSWER_LINE`, clean isolation evidence, the run's dates and the Codex limit snapshots.

## 4. Schedule

- **Order.** The remaining 944 attempts (236 tasks × 2 arms × 2) run in task_id order, four at a time. Each task's four attempts are queued together, so the arms stay interleaved. In the staged attempts, a task's attempts finished within 3 minutes of each other at the median, 5 at most.
- **Codex limits.** The run goes straight through and draws Codex credits past the weekly limit rather than pausing for the reset (2026-10-26T01:53Z). The reports give the dates the attempts ran, each Codex limit snapshot and the credits drawn.
- **Stops.** The runner still stops on a usage or rate limit that ends an attempt without an answer, on an isolation breach, and on uncommitted code, and resumes from where it stopped.
